    Review After the Attempt

    Use these explanations after retrieval practice, or turn on Study Mode for non-answer hints.

    Wireless role map

    Supplicant is the client. The WAP is the authenticator and RADIUS NAS/client. The RADIUS server supports the access decision. AD/LDAP and PKI provide identity evidence behind RADIUS.

    Personal versus Enterprise

    WPA3-Personal uses SAE and a wireless passphrase. WPA3-Enterprise uses 802.1X/EAP and RADIUS. WPA2-Enterprise remains a compatibility option when required clients cannot use WPA3.

    RADIUS secret, identity realm, and wireless password

    The RADIUS shared secret protects WAP-to-RADIUS communication. Wireless users do not enter it. An identity realm scopes or routes identity names; it is neither the shared secret nor automatically the SSID. A WPA-Personal passphrase protects a different relationship.

    PMF and WPA3

    Protected Management Frames help protect deauthentication and disassociation traffic. The WPA3 profiles require PMF.

    Site-survey reasoning

    Choose the least-congested channel compatible with the required band and clients. The familiar 1/6/11 rule applies to the simplified 20-MHz 2.4-GHz exam model, not 5-GHz or 6-GHz planning.

    Hidden SSID: historical versus current

    The historical video required SSID broadcast disabled. That answer is preserved, but hiding an SSID is obscurity rather than effective access control.
